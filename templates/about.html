About HazWaste Sorter AI

Aviation Maintenance Waste Classification Governance & Safety Principles

🎯

1. What the App Does & Does NOT Do

What it does:

HazWaste Sorter is a mobile-first visual aid built for apprentice technicians and engineering students in an aircraft maintenance training hangar in Singapore. It uses Gemini vision models to analyze a photo of discarded workshop materials and suggests the designated waste stream and Singapore hangar bin colour (Red, Yellow, Blue, Black).

What it does NOT do:

  • It does NOT replace the Aircraft Maintenance Manual (AMM) or Standard Operating Procedures (SOP).
  • It does NOT replace National Environment Agency (NEA) toxic industrial waste regulations in Singapore.
  • It does NOT override chemical Safety Data Sheets (SDS) or hangar supervisor directives.
  • It is strictly ADVISORY ONLY.
👷‍♂️

2. The Supervisor & SOP Have the Final Say

In aviation safety, human verification is paramount. If you are uncertain about any material—or if you suspect the AI suggestion is inaccurate—you must immediately consult your hangar workshop supervisor.

Technicians can tap the "Wrong? Report it" button at any time to route any classified item directly into the supervisor review queue.

🛑

3. The 0.70 (70%) Confidence Rule & Why It Exists

Rule: If the AI confidence score is below 0.70 (70%), the system is forbidden from guessing.

Why? Aircraft maintenance involves hazardous and chemically reactive substances (e.g. Skydrol phosphate ester hydraulic fluid, aviation fuels, uncured PRC sealants, and volatile solvents). Guessing can cause chemical cross-contamination, toxic vapor releases, hangar fire hazards, or costly contaminated recycling batches. Below 70% certainty, the app flags the item as REFER TO SUPERVISOR.

🛡️

4. Privacy First: No Faces, Name Tags, or Staff IDs

To protect trainee and staff privacy in accordance with Singapore Personal Data Protection Act (PDPA) standards, photos must show only the waste item.

⚠️ If a person's face, employee name badge, or staff ID card is visible in the photo, the AI immediately rejects the image, instructs you to retake it, and deletes the photo from the device without saving.
⚠️

5. Known Operational Limitations

  • Poor Hangar Lighting: Dark shadows underneath fuselage or wing bays can obscure chemical residue colors and labels.
  • Mixed Waste Items: Multiple different waste materials piled together in a single photo cannot be reliably classified into separate streams.
  • Unlabelled / Crushed Containers: Solvent bottles or sealant tubes with torn, soaked, or missing manufacturer labels cannot be visually distinguished with high certainty.
🗄️

6. Data Storage & 30-Day Retention Policy

Local SQLite Storage: All waste sorting data and audit trails are stored in an on-premise SQLite database (hazwaste.db). Items can never be deleted from the database—only corrected through transparent audit records.

30-Day Photo Deletion Policy: Uploaded waste photos are automatically and permanently deleted from disk after 30 days. The anonymized text audit record (timestamp, item description, chosen stream, and supervisor confirmation) is preserved indefinitely for hangar safety tracking and regulatory NEA compliance.
